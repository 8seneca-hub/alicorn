Product

Five objects. That's the whole product.

Alicorn sits between your repository and your tracker. It doesn't replace either — it records and governs what happens in between. The first object is the one you touch on day one: a workspace, which is a real git worktree with an agent terminal in it. The other four are how you stop supervising them one at a time.

01 · Members

A role you configure once and reuse everywhere

A Member binds four things into a named, reusable entity: the agent backend it runs on, the skills it may use, its permission mode, and whether it works in a git worktree or a plain folder.

That last one is what lets non-engineers in. A business analyst or designer works in a folder workspace with Drive or SharePoint attached, and never needs a repository checkout.

02 · Workflows

Stages, owners and the trigger between them

A workflow is stages in order, the member that owns each one, and what starts the next. Finishing a stage hands work to the next member with no human relay.

04 · The decision trail

The record is the product

Every step writes what ran, what verified it, how much it touched and whether a human was asked. It is what makes removing a checkpoint defensible, and what an auditor reads.

Recorded Used for
Outcome — succeeded or failed Track record for the member on that stage
Verifications — each named check and its result Whether the step qualifies to run unattended
Blast radius — files touched, spend, systems reached The brake. Exceed it and a human is asked.
Gate decision — gated or auto, and the reason The audit answer to "why wasn't I asked?"
Human verdict — accepted, amended or rejected Promotion and demotion of autonomy levels
