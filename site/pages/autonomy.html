Autonomy

The rule that decides whether to interrupt you

"Fewer interruptions" is a promise every agent product makes. What makes it credible is having a rule for which interruption goes away — rather than removing them until something breaks.

Three properties decide it

01

Verifiability

Can a machine tell whether the output is right? Tests, types, lint, build, schema. Absent counts as failed, never as passed.

02

Reversibility

What does undoing it cost? A draft is free. A worktree branch is cheap. A merge to main is not. Authored on the stage, never guessed.

03

Track record

How often has this member, on this kind of step, produced output a human accepted unchanged? Measured over a rolling window, not a lifetime average.

The rule. A step keeps its human gate when it is neither machine-verifiable nor cheaply reversible, or when the member has no track record on it yet. Everything else runs and reports.

Evaluation order

Hard stops are checked first, so no amount of accumulated evidence can retire a gate protecting something irreversible.

evaluateGate(step, policy, evidence):
  policy.mode == 'always_gate'          → gate('policy')
  step.reversibility == 'irreversible'  → gate('irreversible')
  step.inherited_cost == 'high'         → gate('inherited')
  !evidence.all_required_checks_passed  → gate('unverified')
  evidence.files  > policy.max_files    → gate('blast:files')
  evidence.spend  > policy.max_spend    → gate('blast:spend')
  evidence.touched_protected_path       → gate('blast:reach')
  stats.runs        < policy.min_runs   → gate('history')
  stats.accept_rate < policy.min_accept → gate('accept-rate')
  stats.recent_regression               → gate('regression')
  → auto

How a gate retires — and comes back

A stage does not become autonomous by configuration. It earns it, and it can lose it. Both directions are automatic, or the system only ever gets looser.

LevelEntryBehaviour
0 observeddefaultAlways gates. Records the decision it would have made.
1 advisoryruns ≥ 10Gates, but pre-fills a recommendation and measures agreement.
2 conditionalruns ≥ 20 · accept ≥ 90%Auto when verified and inside budget. Gates otherwise.
3 autonomousruns ≥ 50 · accept ≥ 95%Notifies instead of blocking. Hard stops still apply.

Demotion is deliberately blunt. One rejection, or two amendments within the last ten runs, drops a stage one level immediately and requires the full entry condition again. A member with 400 good runs must not average its way out of 12 recent bad ones.

The honest limit

What this rule cannot do

Steps that are neither verifiable nor reversible can only be automated on track record, which is a statistical bet. Those stay gated by default, and we say so rather than pretending otherwise.

Why it counts corrections

If a step passes and someone quietly fixes it later, accept rate would drift up while quality drifts down. Alicorn watches for follow-up commits, reverts and reopened tasks, and counts them against the record.

Start at level zero

Every stage gates on day one. Nothing runs unattended until it has earned it.
