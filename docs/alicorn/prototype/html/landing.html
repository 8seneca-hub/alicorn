The problem

Your agents got fast. Your team got slower.

Developers using AI complete 21% more tasks and merge 98% more pull requests — and then everything jams. Review time is up 441%. Agentic PRs sit 5.3× longer before anyone picks them up. Trust in the output has fallen to 29%.

The reason is specific, and no better model fixes it: a reviewer receives a finished diff with no implementation journey and no decision trail. They have to reconstruct intent from the ticket and the code. So they stay in the loop for everything — approving plans, approving edits, approving commands — because they cannot tell what happened when they weren't watching.

How it works

Set it up once. Then get out of the way.

01

Build your team

Create members with a role, an agent backend, skills and a permission mode. A Developer on Claude Code, a reviewer on Codex, an analyst who never touches the repo.

02

Draw the process

Stages, who owns each, and what triggers the next. Dragging a card to In Review dispatches your reviewer. Findings go back to the author with no human relay.

03

Approve what matters

Everything verifiable and reversible runs on its own. You see a summary. You get interrupted for architecture, merges and deploys — and nothing else.

The autonomy rule

Three gates in eight steps — and not the ones you'd guess.

A step keeps its human gate when it is neither machine-verifiable nor cheaply reversible, or when the member has no track record on it yet. Everything else runs and reports. Gates retire as evidence accumulates, and come back the moment quality slips.
